01

Where are Australia's bees?

Bee records are unevenly distributed across Australia.
These maps show diversity, observations and recording hotspots.

Values are standardised by recording volume.
They are not adjusted for population size.
Occurrence records also reflect recording effort.
Taxa means distinct bee classifications recorded in ALA.
Recording effort means observation and collection activity.

Key takeaway

The ACT shows the highest recorded diversity.
That result is relative to recording effort.
Western Australia and Tasmania also show high richness.

What the maps reveal

Bee records cluster along Australia's coastal regions.
The south-east shows particularly strong recording activity.
These patterns also reflect recording effort.

03

Which bee families dominate the records?

Several families dominate Australia's bee occurrence records.
Their contributions also vary across locations.

Key takeaway

Apidae contributes the largest share of records at 35.5%.
Colletidae, however, contains more unique recorded taxa.
Record volume and taxonomic richness are therefore different.

What the flows reveal

The major bee families occur across multiple states.

04

How does recorded bee diversity compare with managed beekeeping?

Recorded bee diversity and managed hive activity differ.
High diversity does not always mean more hives.

Why this matters

New South Wales has the most managed hives.
Queensland records more unique bee taxa overall.
Higher recorded diversity does not imply more managed hives.

The bigger picture

Bee records vary across geography and seasons.
Queensland records the most unique bee taxa overall.
New South Wales has the most managed hives.
Occurrence records differ from managed beekeeping activity.
