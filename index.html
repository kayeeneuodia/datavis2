Pollinators in Australia

Exploring the geography, seasonality and diversity of Australia's recorded bees, alongside the managed beekeeping industry.

01

Where are Australia's bees?

Bee records are not distributed evenly across Australia. These maps show where recorded diversity is highest, where individual observations occur, and where major recording hotspots emerge.

Occurrence records reflect observation and collection effort and should not be interpreted as estimates of bee population abundance.

02

How does bee activity change through the year?

Recording activity changes substantially across the calendar, and different bee families show different seasonal patterns.

03

Which bee families dominate the records?

Australia's bee occurrence records are dominated by a small number of families, but their contributions differ between states and territories.

Hover over the flows to inspect the state, bee family and occurrence count.

04

How does wild diversity compare with managed beekeeping?

Recorded bee diversity and managed hive activity tell different stories. States with high recorded diversity do not necessarily have the largest managed beekeeping industries.

What does the data tell us?

Australia's recorded bee diversity is geographically and seasonally uneven. Queensland and Western Australia show high recorded taxonomic diversity, while New South Wales has the largest managed hive population among the comparable DAFF regions.

The contrast highlights an important distinction between records of wild bee biodiversity and the distribution of managed beekeeping activity.
