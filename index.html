Bees as Pollinators in Australia

Exploring where Australian bees are recorded and when.
Comparing bee occurrence records with managed beekeeping.

01

Where are Australia's bees?

Bee records are unevenly distributed across Australia.
These maps show diversity, observations and recording hotspots.

Data note: Values are standardised by recording volume and reflect observation effort rather than bee population size.

Key takeaway

ACT has the highest recorded diversity relative to recording effort, while Western Australia and Tasmania also show relatively high richness.

Interactive: Click a bee family in the legend to focus the map.

What the maps reveal

Bee records cluster around coastal Australia, with particularly strong recording activity in the south-east.

02

How does bee activity change through the year?

Bee recording activity changes throughout the calendar.
Different families also show different seasonal patterns.

When are bee families most commonly recorded?

Darker cells show more records. Circles mark each family's peak.

How much does recording activity vary?

Boxes show the middle 50% of months. Dots show individual months.

Each family uses its own vertical scale to emphasise its seasonal pattern.

Seasonal pattern

Apidae and Colletidae peak in November, Halictidae in October, and Megachilidae in January.

03

Which bee families dominate the records?

Several families dominate Australia's bee occurrence records.
Their contributions also vary across locations.

Key takeaway

Apidae has the largest share of records at 35.5%, while Colletidae contains more unique recorded taxa.

Interactive: Hover over a flow to compare state, family and record count.

What the flows reveal

Major bee families occur across multiple states, but their relative contribution changes geographically.

04

How does recorded bee diversity compare with managed beekeeping?

Recorded bee diversity and managed hive activity differ. High diversity does not always mean more hives.

Interactive: Click a state in the legend to focus the comparison.

Why this matters

New South Wales has the most managed hives, while Queensland records more unique bee taxa overall.

The bigger picture

Bee records vary across geography and seasons. Queensland records the most unique bee taxa overall, while New South Wales has the most managed hives.
